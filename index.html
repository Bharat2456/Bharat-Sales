How it works

From empty list to booked call

Four steps. You stay in control of everything that leaves your inbox.

Describe your business

What you sell, who buys, your website, and the hours you are free for calls.

Find and read

Search the map or paste a list. The platform reads each website and scores how well they fit.

Approve and send

Review each draft, then open it in Gmail or your mail app and press send.

Reply and book

Paste replies back. Get a drafted answer with your free times, then a calendar file.

Features

Everything between “who” and “booked”

One place for the whole outreach loop, without a stack of tools.

Find businesses

Search a city or district on the map for one type of business, or paste your own list. Directories and duplicates are dropped for you.

Read their website

Fetches the homepage, contact and about pages, lifts emails and phone numbers by rule, and has the AI note what they do. Nothing is kept unless it appears in the text.

Write the first email

A short, plain email that refers to something real on their site. A built-in check blocks banned phrases and missing opt-outs before you can approve it.

Follow-ups on a schedule

If nobody replies, the next nudge is drafted when it is due, with gaps you set. Replies and opt-outs stop the sequence.

Handle replies

Paste what they wrote. The platform sorts it: interested, wants a call, not now, opt-out, auto-reply. Then it drafts the answer for you to review.

Book the call

You enter the hours you are free once. When someone wants to talk, the reply offers real slots, avoids your busy times, and gives a calendar file when booked.

Straight answers

What it does, and what it leaves to you

Outreach tools overpromise. Here is exactly how this one behaves.

It does

  • Find named businesses by type and place from OpenStreetMap
  • Read public web pages and pull out contact details
  • Score fit, draft emails and follow-ups, and triage replies
  • Offer your real free times and produce a calendar file
  • Keep everything in your browser, with backup export and import

It leaves to you

  • Pressing send: emails open in Gmail or your mail app, nothing is sent behind your back
  • Checking addresses: emails are not verified, so keep your volume modest
  • Complete coverage: map data is thin in some regions, and many small businesses list no website
  • Your AI bill: you use your own key and pay your provider directly
  • Legal compliance for your region: you decide who to contact
Privacy

No accounts. No database. No middleman for your key.

Leads and settings are stored in your browser. Your AI key goes from your browser straight to the provider you choose. The only server piece is a small function that fetches a public web page for you, and it refuses private network addresses.

Bring your own key

Anthropic, OpenAI, Gemini or OpenRouter.

Safety checks built in

Opt-out lines, do-not-contact list, banned phrases and a pre-send gate.

See the pipeline

FAQ

Questions

Does it send the emails itself?

No. It opens each approved email in Gmail or your mail app, fully written. You press send. That keeps your sender reputation and your judgment in your hands.

Do I need an account?

No. There is no sign-up. Your data lives in this browser, so export a backup from Settings if you switch devices.

Which AI can I use?

Anthropic, OpenAI, Google Gemini or OpenRouter. Paste a key in Settings and press Test connection.

How does it find businesses?

It searches OpenStreetMap for a business type inside a city or district. You can also paste a list from a directory or your own sheet.

How does scheduling work?

You enter the days and hours you are free, your time zone and call length. When a reply asks for a call, the drafted answer offers up to three real slots and avoids times you already booked.

Is it really free and open source?

Yes. It is released under the GNU AGPL-3.0. If you run a modified version for others, the licence asks you to share your changes.
